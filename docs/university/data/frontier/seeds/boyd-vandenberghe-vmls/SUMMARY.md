## What it is

The free PDF of the textbook *Introduction to Applied Linear Algebra: Vectors, Matrices, and Least Squares* (subtitle "VMLS") by **Stephen Boyd** (Department of Electrical Engineering, Stanford University) and **Lieven Vandenberghe** (Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, UCLA). Copyright is held by Cambridge University Press, which has agreed to let the authors keep the book available on the web free of charge — so this is a legally free textbook, not a pirated copy (per the companion page, retrieved 2026-10-07). It is the course text for Stanford ENGR108 and UCLA EE133A. The companion page also offers lecture slides, lecture videos, a Julia companion, extra exercises, and links to community Python/Julia notebooks.

## Actual subject

Applied linear algebra aimed at beginners with little or no prior exposure: vectors, matrices, and least-squares methods, taught through the lens of modern applications (data fitting, machine learning/AI, tomography, navigation, image processing, finance, automatic control). No probability or statistics background required; calculus used only lightly and is not a strict prerequisite.

## Key topics (from the table of contents — factual metadata, not reproduced prose)

- **Part I — Vectors (Ch 1–5):** vectors and notation; linear functions and Taylor approximation; regression models; norm, distance, standard deviation, angle; clustering and the k-means algorithm; linear independence, bases, orthonormal vectors, Gram–Schmidt.
- **Part II — Matrices (Ch 6–11):** matrix basics, transpose/addition/norm; matrix examples (geometric transforms, selectors, incidence matrices, convolution); linear equations; linear dynamical systems (population, epidemic, motion, supply-chain models); matrix multiplication and QR factorization; matrix inverses and the pseudo-inverse.
- **Part III — Least squares (Ch 12–19):** the least-squares problem and its solution; data fitting with validation and feature engineering; least-squares classification (incl. multi-class); multi-objective least squares; constrained least squares; constrained-LS applications — portfolio optimization, linear quadratic control, linear quadratic state estimation; nonlinear least squares (Gauss–Newton, Levenberg–Marquardt); constrained nonlinear least squares (penalty and augmented Lagrangian methods).
- **Appendices:** notation, computational complexity, derivatives/optimization/Lagrange multipliers, further study.

## Pedagogical stance (paraphrased from the preface)

The book practices "intellectual economy": one theoretical concept (linear independence), one computational tool (QR factorization), and one method family (least squares and its extensions) carry the whole applications arc. Programming is encouraged but not required — exercises that need computation are optional, with data files and language-specific resources online.
