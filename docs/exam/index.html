Scenario exam

Score. Remediate. Repeat.

Realistic interview and on-call scenarios across networking, Kubernetes, DevOps, and cloud — each scored instantly, each miss remediated on the spot with an explanation, an adaptive drill path, and a Socratic coach session. This is not trivia: every scenario is a situation a hiring manager could actually hand you. Take it below.

How the exam works

The format

Each question is a scenario, not a vocabulary check. A VPC needs a subnet carved to spec. A Deployment is in CrashLoopBackOff. The stem describes the situation; the four options describe candidate actions; only one of them is what a competent engineer does first. The exam draws from operational domains — subnetting, container orchestration, pipelines, incident triage — because those are the questions that separate a resume from a hire. Your answers are scored locally in your browser, with your history kept in local storage so a re-take shows whether remediation actually worked.

The scoring

Every answer is judged right or wrong, but a wrong answer is never left hanging: each scenario carries a why — the reasoning that makes the correct action correct — and the exam surfaces it immediately. There is no credential on the other side of a passing score, and the page will not claim one; what it gives you is something a certificate can't: the exact set of scenarios you missed, with the reasoning attached. That miss list is the most valuable thing on the page — it is your next study plan, written by your own mistakes.

The remediation

A miss is a fork, not a dead end. Every scenario ships two remediation routes: an adaptive drill on the adaptive path, which feeds the missed domain into the ready-to-learn fringe, and a Socratic session with Noah pre-loaded with the scenario's exact topic — never an answer dump first, tips and reasoning before solutions. Take the remediation, then come back and re-take: score, remediate, repeat until the miss list is empty. That's the whole system, and it's the whole point of the page.
