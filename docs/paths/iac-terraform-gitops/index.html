Role loop

Think like the platform engineer who owns the repo everyone deploys from: model → plan → review → apply → reconcile. Every phase trains that loop: declare the infrastructure, preview the change, get it reviewed, ship it, and keep reality matching the code.

Gamified mastery modules — four phases

Sixteen modules in four gated phases — what you learn, and why it matters in production or in an interview. Each phase ends with a gate: quiz, hands-on lab, timed sim, and oral defense.

  1. Phase 1

    Foundations — the IaC mindset

    • Declarative thinking

      Desired state vs. imperative steps, and idempotence. Why it matters: this mental shift is the foundation everything else rests on.

    • HCL & providers

      Syntax, provider blocks, and version constraints. Why it matters: readable code is reviewable infrastructure — write for the next human.

    • State

      What state is, why it matters, and remote backends. Why it matters: state is the source of truth and the biggest footgun in Terraform.

    • CLI workflow

      init, plan, apply, and destroy. Why it matters: plan-before-apply is the discipline that keeps Friday deploys boring.

  2. Phase 2

    Core — writing real Terraform

    • Resources & data sources

      Modeling real infrastructure as a resource graph. Why it matters: everything in Terraform is a node in a graph — think in dependencies.

    • Variables & outputs

      Inputs, locals, and outputs with clean interfaces. Why it matters: reusable code needs contracts, not copy-paste.

    • Modules

      Writing and consuming modules. Why it matters: modules are how teams scale IaC without every engineer reinventing the VPC.

    • Workspaces & environments

      Dev, stage, and prod patterns. Why it matters: environment parity is what prevents “works on my laptop” infrastructure.

  3. Phase 3

    Advanced — teams and guardrails

    • Remote state & locking

      Backends, state locking, and team workflows. Why it matters: teams share state or they share pain — pick one deliberately.

    • Testing & policy

      Validation, linting, and policy-as-code literacy. Why it matters: guardrails beat heroics; catch the bad plan before it applies.

    • Terraform in CI

      Plan on pull request, apply on merge. Why it matters: this is how infrastructure changes get reviewed like code — because they are code.

    • Drift & imports

      Detecting drift and importing existing resources. Why it matters: brownfield is the real world — greenfield is the demo.

  4. Phase 4

    Proof — GitOps end to end

    • GitOps with Argo CD

      App-of-apps patterns and sync waves. Why it matters: Git as the source of truth is how modern teams deliver to clusters.

    • End-to-end pipeline

      Terraform plus GitOps delivering a real stack. Why it matters: the full loop — provision then deliver — in one demoable story.

    • Drift remediation

      Detect a drifted environment, explain it, reconcile it. Why it matters: the plan said one thing and reality said another — now what?

    • Evidence artifacts

      Module READMEs and architecture decision records. Why it matters: interviews are won with stories, and stories need artifacts.

Assessments

Assessment here is evidence, not watch-time. Each phase ends with a gate: a short adaptive quiz that finds your weak spots, a SuperLab task lab graded on outcome (did the thing actually work?), and a timed troubleshooting sim where you diagnose a broken system against the clock. Missed concepts remediate into /adapt/ fringe sessions instead of failing you outright. IaC review drills and pipeline sims live in the drill library. Oral defense matters too: after every phase you explain a past failure out loud in STAR format, because interviews are oral exams.

Capstone

Capstone: “Platform in a repo.” Build one versioned repository that provisions a complete environment — network, compute, and data — with Terraform, then delivers applications onto it via GitOps. Wire CI so pull requests show plans and merges apply them, add drift detection with a documented remediation runbook, and prove the teardown leaves nothing behind. Graduate with the repo, the pipeline, the ADRs explaining your choices, and a demo you can walk an interviewer through live.

FAQ

What should I know before starting?

Basic Linux shell comfort and a general idea of what cloud infrastructure is. No prior Terraform or GitOps needed.

How long does it take?

Self-paced. The four phases are sequential and gated — advance when you clear each gate, on whatever schedule your life allows.

Is this a HashiCorp certification?

No. This is independent preparation built by Interstitium Labs — not affiliated with or endorsed by HashiCorp, and it confers no certificate. It builds the real workflow skills vendor credentials test.

Are these real exam questions?

No. All quizzes, drills, and sims are original practice material. We never reproduce exam questions.

What do I need to run the labs?

Terraform and a GitOps-capable cluster target — kind locally or SuperLab. Cloud-provider labs can use free-tier accounts with budget alarms.
