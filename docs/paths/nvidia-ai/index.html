Suggested 10-week learning plan

Sustainable pace: roughly 2–4 hours/week (longer weeks for the 8-hour labs). Adjust freely.

Phase 1 · Foundations (Weeks 1–3)

  • Week 1 — Generative AI Explained + Building A Brain in 10 Minutes
  • Week 2 — Agentic AI Explained + Beginner's Guide to Autonomous Robots
  • Week 3 — Review notes; skim the DLI free catalog for adjacent interests

Phase 2 · Applied skills (Weeks 4–7)

  • Week 4 — Data Science Workflows with Zero Code Changes (RAPIDS)
  • Week 5 — Generative AI in Digital Health
  • Week 6 — AI Infrastructure and Operations Fundamentals (split across the week if needed)
  • Week 7 — Developing with NVIDIA Omniverse

Phase 3 · Advanced development (Weeks 8–10)

  • Week 8 — Jetson Nano AI (part 1)
  • Week 9 — Jetson Nano AI (part 2) + first edge project artifact
  • Week 10 — Building RAG Agents with LLMs

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

  • Are these NVIDIA's courses or yours?
    NVIDIA's. This page is a curated catalog and track over NVIDIA's free AI courses — the courses themselves live with NVIDIA, and certificate issuance is theirs.
  • Do the short courses grant certificates?
    Not always: short awareness courses may not issue DLI certificates. The catalog says so explicitly — confirm on NVIDIA's page before relying on a badge for anything.
  • Where should I start?
    Generative AI Explained: beginner level, about two hours, DLI certificate issued. Concepts with no coding — the on-ramp before anything agentic or mathematical.
  • “Building a Brain in 10 Minutes” — really ten minutes?
    Approximately — neural network fundamentals in a micro-format, likely DLI-certified. Durations across the catalog are approximate; treat them as sizing, not promises.
  • Are the durations and certificate notes exact?
    No — the page states they are approximate and to confirm on NVIDIA before relying on a badge. Course catalogs change; the track is honest about that.
  • What is the track's goal?
    An AI-literacy ladder: concepts first, then agentic AI, then deeper material — sequenced so each course assumes only what the previous ones taught.

Concept glossary

The words this track uses

  • DLI — NVIDIA Deep Learning Institute: the issuer of the certificates behind most courses in this catalog; certificate issuance is theirs, not ours.
  • Generative AI — models that produce text, images, or code from prompts; the track's on-ramp course ("Generative AI Explained") teaches the concepts with no coding.
  • Agentic AI — systems where models plan and act through tools; covered by the beginner/intermediate "Agentic AI Explained" course.
  • RAG — retrieval-augmented generation: grounding a model's answers in retrieved documents; the advanced capstone course ("Building RAG Agents with LLMs").
  • RAPIDS — NVIDIA's GPU-accelerated data-science libraries; the subject of the "Data Science Workflows with Zero Code Changes" course.
  • AI Quest — the lightweight local-only tracker beside this page (XP, levels, badges, streak); progress stays in your browser's localStorage.
  • DevOps crossover — the AI Infrastructure and Operations Fundamentals course (GPU clusters, data-center ops, platform literacy) that interviewers increasingly probe on DevOps screens.

Worked procedure

Run the 10-week track, start to finish

  1. Start at the on-ramp. Take "Generative AI Explained" (beginner, ~2 hours, DLI certificate) plus "Building A Brain in 10 Minutes" in week 1 — concepts before anything agentic or mathematical.
  2. Work the phases in order. Foundations (weeks 1–3) → applied skills (weeks 4–7) → advanced development (weeks 8–10), at roughly 2–4 hours a week; split the 8-hour courses across weeks.
  3. Verify certificates on NVIDIA. Durations and certificate notes here are approximate — confirm on NVIDIA's page before relying on any badge, since short awareness courses may not issue DLI certificates.
  4. Prioritize the DevOps crossover. If you are on the a-desk interview sprint or the 12-month DevSecOps mastery, take AI Infrastructure and Operations Fundamentals seriously — GPU and data-center literacy now shows up in DevOps screens.
  5. Track progress locally. Log each finished course in the NVIDIA AI Quest tracker; streaks and badges live in your browser only.

Project brief

The applied brief

Deliverable: one edge project artifact (from the Jetson Nano weeks) and one RAG agent prototype (from the final course) — built locally, documented with a README that states what the model does and where it fails.

Acceptance: both artifacts run end to end on your hardware; you can explain the model's limits without hand-waving. Certificates are NVIDIA's; the artifacts are yours.
