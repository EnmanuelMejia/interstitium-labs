Role loop

Think like a cluster admin on call: diagnose → declare → deploy → observe → secure → recover. Every phase of this path exercises that loop — you read a broken system, declare the desired state, ship it, watch it, lock it down, and prove you can bring it back.

Gamified mastery modules — four phases

Sixteen modules in four gated phases. Each module states what you learn and why it matters on call or in an interview. Clear each phase gate — quiz, lab, timed sim, oral defense — to advance.

  1. Phase 1

    Foundations — the machine under the cluster

    • Linux & container primitives

      Namespaces, cgroups, and images vs. containers. Why it matters: every strange pod behavior on call bottoms out at the Linux layer, and interviewers can tell who skipped it.

    • Cluster anatomy

      API server, etcd, scheduler, controller manager, kubelet, kube-proxy, and the container runtime. Why it matters: you cannot fix — or explain — what you cannot draw from memory.

    • kubectl fluency

      Imperative create/run/expose, output formats, and jsonpath basics. Why it matters: timed drills are won at the keyboard, not by copy-pasting YAML templates.

    • Declarative manifests

      Hand-written Pod, ReplicaSet, and Deployment YAML. Why it matters: GitOps starts with manifests a teammate can read and review.

  2. Phase 2

    Core — running real workloads

    • Workloads in depth

      Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets, Jobs, and CronJobs. Why it matters: picking the wrong controller is how production breaks at 2 AM.

    • Services, DNS & ingress

      ClusterIP, NodePort, LoadBalancer, CoreDNS, and Ingress rules. Why it matters: “the app is up but unreachable” is the most common ticket you will ever see.

    • Config & secrets

      ConfigMaps and Secrets mounted as env or volumes. Why it matters: leaked secrets end careers; this is where operational discipline starts.

    • Scheduling

      Node selectors, affinity and anti-affinity, taints and tolerations. Why it matters: placement control is the line between “it runs” and “it survives.”

  3. Phase 3

    Advanced — security, storage, observability

    • RBAC & service accounts

      Roles, ClusterRoles, and bindings with least privilege. Why it matters: production power flows through RBAC, and auditors ask about it first.

    • Network policy & pod security

      Default-deny posture and Pod Security Standards. Why it matters: this is where interviewers probe whether your security thinking is real.

    • Storage

      PersistentVolumes, claims, and StorageClasses with dynamic provisioning. Why it matters: stateful apps are where “just delete the pod” stops working.

    • Observability

      Liveness, readiness, and startup probes, log triage, and metrics-server. Why it matters: you cannot claim reliability without proving what is failing.

  4. Phase 4

    Proof — operate under pressure

    • Troubleshooting drills

      CrashLoopBackOff, ImagePullBackOff, and Pending pods diagnosed against the clock. Why it matters: timed diagnosis is the whole game in admin interviews.

    • Cluster operations

      kubeadm upgrades and etcd backup/restore. Why it matters: an admin who cannot restore etcd cannot own a cluster.

    • GitOps handoff

      Walking an Argo CD sync story on SuperLab. Why it matters: declarative delivery is how teams actually ship to clusters today.

    • Evidence portfolio

      STAR write-ups per domain with blast-radius analysis. Why it matters: interviews are won with stories, and stories need artifacts.

Assessments

Here, assessment is evidence, not watch-time. Each phase ends with a gate: a short adaptive quiz that finds your weak spots, a SuperLab task lab graded on outcome (did the thing actually work?), and a timed troubleshooting sim where you diagnose a broken system against the clock. Missed concepts remediate into /adapt/ fringe sessions instead of failing you outright. Timed cluster sims live in the drill library alongside desk-style Q&A. After every phase you also explain a past failure out loud in STAR format — interviews are oral exams.

Capstone

Capstone: “Midnight pager.” You inherit a SuperLab cluster with six injected faults: a CrashLoopBackOff deployment, a misconfigured NetworkPolicy cutting off the frontend, an expired certificate on the API server path, a full etcd disk, a broken HPA, and a secret committed in plaintext. Diagnose and fix all six, harden the cluster (RBAC least-privilege plus default-deny network policy), snapshot etcd, and deliver the fix as a GitOps-managed change with a written postmortem. Graduate with the postmortem, the hardened manifests, and a STAR story for every fault.

FAQ

What should I know before starting?

A comfortable Linux shell and basic container ideas (image vs. container). If Docker is new, start with the Docker containers sequence linked in the map above.

How long does it take?

Self-paced. Learners move through the four phases in order; the page's spine marker suggests a multi-week arc, but there are no deadlines — gates, not calendars, decide when you advance.

Is this the CKA exam?

No. This is independent preparation built by Interstitium Labs — not affiliated with the Linux Foundation or CNCF, and it confers no certificate. It prepares you to earn the credential elsewhere and to defend the skills in interviews.

Are these real exam questions?

No. Every quiz, drill, and sim is original practice material. We never reproduce exam questions.

What do I need to run the labs?

A machine that can run kind or k3d locally, or access to SuperLab. Everything else is in the lesson sequences.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

  • What is the CKA actually testing?
    Cluster operations under time pressure: workloads and scheduling, services and network policy, storage and config, troubleshooting. The phases mirror that reality — the exam is a performance, not a quiz.
  • Why “exceed”?
    The IL layer adds Adapt gates, Noah Socratic coaching, and SuperLab proof on every phase — while honestly stating what we are not: the official exam, its labs, and its credential stay with the Linux Foundation.
  • How am I assessed here?
    By operating under pressure: timed troubleshooting, broken clusters to fix, policies to enforce. “Proof — operate under pressure” is the final phase because the CKA rewards hands, not highlights.
  • Does this grant the CKA?
    No. This is CKA-shaped readiness with lab evidence. The Certified Kubernetes Administrator credential is conferred by the Linux Foundation after their exam.
  • What is the GitOps handoff phase?
    The transition from imperative kubectl to declarative GitOps: your cluster stops being a pet you SSH into and becomes desired state in Git. It is where the path hands you to real-world workflows.
  • What do I need before starting?
    Containers fluency — the docker-containers sequence is the prerequisite lane. Six to ten weeks assumes you can already think in images and layers.
