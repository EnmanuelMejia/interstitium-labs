Exercise brief

District challenge: enrollment and related signals are not analyzed consistently across schools, HR, and other departments. The role would help build that capacity — HR metrics, staffing trends, compliance reporting — as an individual contributor.

Task: using public LPS enrollment on DESE, prepare a 15-minute presentation with multi-year trends, visualizations, planning/DEIB/resourcing insights, and explicit assumptions/limitations.

Focus areas: race/ethnicity · gender · grade · ELL · students with disabilities (SPED) · economically disadvantaged.

Slide outline (15 min)

  1. 1. Title — Enrollment trends & strategic insights (2018–2023 framing).
  2. 2. Panel — Acknowledge HR, strategic planning, CTO, and academic leadership priorities.
  3. 3. Agenda — Trends → demographics → implications → limits → next steps.
  4. 4. Enrollment overview — Total enrollment direction; elementary vs high-school contrast.
  5. 5. Race/ethnicity — Composition shift; culturally responsive staffing.
  6. 6. ELL — Growth and language-support capacity.
  7. 7. SPED / disabilities — Rising need; specialized hiring.
  8. 8. Economically disadvantaged — Stable high need; funding & wrap-around services.
  9. 9. Cross-cuts — DEIB + HR + technology (dashboards).
  10. 10. Assumptions & limits — Self-report categories, missing years, no mobility file.
  11. 11. Questions / next steps — Peer-district compare, staffing ratios, live dashboard.
  12. 12. Close — Q&A.

Gender and grade-level charts sit inside demographics; graduation / MCAS slides in an attached draft deck were flagged as outside the DESE enrollment extract unless separately sourced.

Key insights (from the working thread)

Treat as hypotheses pending DESE refresh:

  • Multi-year growth narrative with elementary (K–5) expansion and softer high-school counts → HR should weight K–5 hiring and room capacity.
  • Hispanic/Latino majority deepening → bilingual staff, culturally responsive curriculum, family engagement in Spanish.
  • ELL share rising (thread cited large %-growth and a high Spanish share) → ESL capacity, bilingual recruitment, counselor language access.
  • SPED share up; autism category called out as faster-growing within SPED → specialized educators and training budget.
  • Economically disadvantaged remains very high (thread ~high-70s%) and correlates with ELL growth → Title funding advocacy and community partnerships.
  • Gender roughly balanced; non-binary reporting incomplete → note data gaps rather than over-interpret.
  • Operational ask: one enrollment dashboard shared by schools, HR, and planning — reduce “data that never gets pulled.”

Assumptions & limitations

  • DESE categories include self-reported fields; definitions (e.g., economically disadvantaged) follow state rules and can change.
  • Absolute counts vs percentages can tell different stories when totals move — both should be shown.
  • No student-level mobility / transfer file in the public profile extract.
  • External shocks (pandemic, housing, immigration policy) are not in the table but may drive trends.
  • 2024–25 visuals in the AI share were hypothetical / example — not official DESE.
  • Graduation, MCAS, and student–teacher ratio require other DESE reports if retained in a deck.

What this demonstrates

  • Scoping a 15-minute stakeholder talk for HR + strategy + technology.
  • Turning public education data into DEIB, workforce, and resourcing narratives.
  • Naming assumptions and refusing to pretend incomplete years are measured.
  • Using AI as a drafting partner while keeping human verification against the source of record (DESE).
