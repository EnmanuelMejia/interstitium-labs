MODULE 1: m22-approaching — Approaching the Traditions
SUMMARY: How to study esoteric traditions honestly: emic vs. etic reading, the perennial problems of sources and lineages, and the stance this course takes throughout.
OBJECTIVES:
  - Distinguish emic (insider) from etic (scholarly) readings
  - Explain why esoteric lineages are hard to verify
  - State this course's framing contract
  - Read a primary esoteric text descriptively without credulity or contempt
NOTES:
  [TITLE] The framing contract
  Everything in this course is presented **as the traditions themselves present it**, in a scholarly and descriptive tone. A statement like 'the Kabbalistic tradition teaches...' describes a tradition's claims — it does not assert them as verified fact. Esoteric content here is **history, symbolism, and practice as claimed by its practitioners**, never presented as established science.
  [TITLE] Emic vs. etic
  **Emic** reading takes the tradition's own categories seriously (what does the Tree of Life *mean* within Kabbalah?). **Etic** reading stands outside (what social functions did alchemy serve in 16th-century courts?). Both are legitimate; confusing them produces either credulity or contempt. This course moves between the two deliberately and labels which is in play.
  [TITLE] The lineage problem
  Esoteric traditions routinely claim ancient origins — and scholarship routinely finds the texts are younger than claimed. The *Corpus Hermeticum* was dated to Moses' era by Renaissance readers; Isaac Casaubon's philology (1614) placed it in the 2nd–3rd centuries CE. **Neither the claim nor the debunking is the whole story**: study what the tradition *did* with its claimed antiquity.
  [TITLE] How to work this course
  Learn each system's **internal logic** first — its symbols, cosmology, and practices as a coherent whole. Then ask the historical questions: who wrote this, when, for whom, to what end? The quizzes test knowledge *of* the traditions: what practitioners claim, what scholars have established, and the difference between the two.
CARDS:
  F: This course's framing contract in one sentence?
  B: Traditions are presented as the traditions present themselves — descriptive and scholarly, never as verified science.
  F: Emic vs. etic reading?
  B: Emic: inside the tradition's own categories. Etic: outside, scholarly/historical analysis.
  F: Casaubon's 1614 finding about the Corpus Hermeticum?
  B: Philological dating to the 2nd–3rd centuries CE — not the Mosaic antiquity Renaissance readers assumed.
  F: The right order for studying a tradition?
  B: Internal logic first (symbols, cosmology, practice as a whole), then historical-critical questions.

  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: The Casaubon pattern: the interesting question is what the tradition built on its claimed antiquity.

MODULE 2: m22-kabbalah — Kabbalah & Jewish Mysticism
SUMMARY: The Tree of Life, the ten sefirot, the Zohar, and Lurianic cosmology — the central symbolic system of Jewish esotericism, as its tradition presents it.
OBJECTIVES:
  - Name the ten sefirot and their arrangement on the Tree
  - Describe the Zohar's place in the tradition
  - Summarize Luria's tzimtzum, shevirah, and tikkun
  - Distinguish classical Kabbalah from later popularizations
NOTES:
  [TITLE] The Tree of Life and the sefirot
  Classical Kabbalah maps divine emanation as ten **sefirot** arranged in three pillars on the **Tree of Life**: Keter (crown), Chokhmah (wisdom), Binah (understanding), Chesed (loving-kindness), Gevurah (strength/judgment), Tiferet (beauty/balance), Netzach (endurance), Hod (splendor), Yesod (foundation), Malkhut (kingship). Twenty-two paths — associated with the Hebrew letters — connect them. The Tree is simultaneously cosmology, psychology, and a map of the divine, in the tradition's own terms.
  [TITLE] The Zohar
  The *Zohar* ('Book of Splendor'), the central text of classical Kabbalah, appeared in 13th-century Spain through **Moses de León**, who attributed it to the 2nd-century sage Shimon bar Yochai — an attribution scholars do not accept as historical. Written largely in Aramaic as a mystical commentary on the Torah, it systematized sefirotic symbolism and became canonical for later Kabbalists.
  [TITLE] Luria and the cosmic drama
  **Isaac Luria** (1534–1572) of Safed taught a mythic cosmology his disciples recorded: **tzimtzum** (divine contraction making space for creation), **shevirat ha-kelim** (the shattering of the vessels, scattering holy sparks), and **tikkun** (restoration through human action). In Lurianic teaching, ordinary ethical and ritual acts gather the sparks — exile and redemption rendered as cosmic physics.
  [TITLE] Classical vs. popular Kabbalah
  Classical Kabbalah was an elite, textually demanding discipline within Judaism, historically restricted (traditionally to mature, learned students). Modern popularizations — from 19th-century occultist adaptations to celebrity spiritualities — often borrow the Tree's diagram while discarding its textual and communal matrix. Knowing the difference is basic literacy in the field.
CARDS:
  F: Name the ten sefirot.
  B: Keter, Chokhmah, Binah, Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, Malkhut.
  F: The Zohar: when, through whom, attributed to whom?
  B: 13th-century Spain via Moses de León; attributed to Shimon bar Yochai (2nd c.) — attribution not accepted by scholars.
  F: Luria's three-stage cosmology?
  B: Tzimtzum (contraction), shevirah (shattering of vessels), tikkun (restoration).
  F: What do the 22 paths on the Tree correspond to?
  B: The 22 letters of the Hebrew alphabet, in the tradition's symbolism.
  F: Classical vs. popular Kabbalah?
  B: Classical: elite textual discipline within Judaism. Popular: later borrowings, often diagram-only.

MODULE 3: m22-hermeticism — Hermeticism & the Corpus Hermeticum
SUMMARY: Hermes Trismegistus, the Hermetic writings, and the seven Hermetic principles as formulated in the modern Hermetic tradition.
OBJECTIVES:
  - Describe the Corpus Hermeticum's contents and dating
  - State the seven Hermetic principles in the tradition's terms
  - Explain 'as above, so below' in context
  - Trace Hermeticism's Renaissance revival
NOTES:
  [TITLE] The writings and their date
  The **Corpus Hermeticum** is a collection of Greek dialogues (notably the *Poimandres*) attributed to **Hermes Trismegistus** ('thrice-greatest Hermes'), a syncretic figure fusing the Greek Hermes and Egyptian Thoth. Renaissance scholars took them for primeval Egyptian wisdom; **Isaac Casaubon's** 1614 philological analysis dated the texts to the 2nd–3rd centuries CE — Hellenistic Egypt, not pharaonic antiquity.
  [TITLE] The seven principles (modern formulation)
  The **seven Hermetic principles** as commonly listed in the modern Hermetic tradition (popularized by *The Kybalion*, 1908, published anonymously as 'Three Initiates'): **Mentalism** (all is mind), **Correspondence** (as above, so below), **Vibration** (nothing rests), **Polarity** (opposites are degrees of the same), **Rhythm** (all flows in and out), **Cause and Effect** (every cause has its effect), **Gender** (masculine and feminine principles in all things). These are the tradition's claims, presented as such.
  [TITLE] 'As above, so below'
  The **Principle of Correspondence** — 'as above, so below; as below, so above' (from the *Emerald Tablet*, a short Hermetic text of uncertain date) — asserts patterned resonance between levels of reality: cosmos and psyche, macrocosm and microcosm. In the tradition's use it is a *hermeneutic* principle (read one level through another), not an empirical prediction.
  [TITLE] Renaissance revival
  Marsilio Ficino's Latin translation (1463–1471, commissioned by Cosimo de' Medici) made Hermes a founding authority of Renaissance Platonism — read alongside Plato as ancient theology (*prisca theologia*). Hermetic ideas then flowed into alchemy, early science (Newton owned alchemical manuscripts), and eventually the 19th-century occult revival.
CARDS:
  F: Who is Hermes Trismegistus?
  B: A syncretic figure fusing Greek Hermes and Egyptian Thoth; attributed author of the Hermetic writings.
  F: Casaubon's 1614 dating of the Corpus?
  B: 2nd–3rd centuries CE (Hellenistic Egypt), not pharaonic antiquity.
  F: List the seven Hermetic principles.
  B: Mentalism, Correspondence, Vibration, Polarity, Rhythm, Cause and Effect, Gender (modern formulation via The Kybalion, 1908).
  F: 'As above, so below' — source and meaning?
  B: The Emerald Tablet; the Principle of Correspondence — patterned resonance between levels, used hermeneutically.
  F: Ficino's translation (1460s): significance?
  B: Made Hermes a founding authority of Renaissance Platonism (prisca theologia).

     2) The future can be predicted from the past
     3) Mind and matter are identical substances
  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: Correspondence = the macrocosm/microcosm resonance, the tradition's hermeneutic key.
  Q: The seven-principles list comes to the modern tradition primarily via:

MODULE 4: m22-alchemy — Alchemy — History & Symbolism
SUMMARY: From Hellenistic Egypt to Paracelsus: transmutation, the philosopher's stone, and the symbolic language of solve et coagula.
OBJECTIVES:
  - Trace alchemy's historical arc across cultures
  - Decode core symbols: solve et coagula, the stone, the stages
  - Explain alchemy's relationship to early chemistry
  - Read an alchemical emblem descriptively
NOTES:
  [TITLE] The historical arc
  Alchemy begins in **Hellenistic Egypt** (Zosimos of Panopolis, ~300 CE), flowers in the **Islamic world** (Jabir ibn Hayyan's corpus, 8th–9th c. — experimental technique and theory), enters **medieval Europe** via translation (12th c.), and peaks in the **Renaissance** (Paracelsus, 1493–1541: medicine and the *tria prima*). It fades as chemistry's quantitative program — Boyle, Lavoisier — replaces qualitative transmutation theory in the 17th–18th centuries.
  [TITLE] The Great Work
  The **Magnum Opus** (Great Work) aimed at the **philosopher's stone**: the agent of transmutation of base metals into gold and of bodily perfection (the elixir of life). The work proceeds through color-stages — **nigredo** (blackening, putrefaction), **albedo** (whitening, purification), **citrinitas** (yellowing), **rubedo** (reddening, completion). Practitioners read these as material *and* spiritual operations simultaneously.
  [TITLE] Solve et coagula
  **Solve et coagula** ('dissolve and coagulate'): the master formula — break the substance down to its prima materia, then reconstitute it in perfected form. In the tradition's symbolic reading, the same operation applies to the practitioner: dissolution of the ego, reconstitution of the self. The motto governs both laboratory and contemplative alchemy.
  [TITLE] Alchemy and chemistry
  Alchemy bequeathed real technique: distillation, sublimation, crystallization, and laboratory apparatus — plus figures like **Paracelsus** who founded iatrochemistry (medical chemistry). Newton's extensive alchemical manuscripts show the boundary was porous. Modern scholarship reads alchemy as proto-chemistry *and* as a spiritual discipline — the two were not separate projects for its practitioners.
CARDS:
  F: Alchemy's historical stations?
  B: Hellenistic Egypt (Zosimos) → Islamic world (Jabir) → medieval Europe → Renaissance (Paracelsus) → displaced by quantitative chemistry.
  F: The four color-stages of the Great Work?
  B: Nigredo (blackening), albedo (whitening), citrinitas (yellowing), rubedo (reddening).
  F: Solve et coagula?
  B: 'Dissolve and coagulate' — break down to prima materia, reconstitute perfected; applied to matter and self alike.
  F: Paracelsus: contribution?
  B: The tria prima (sulfur, mercury, salt) and iatrochemistry — alchemy turned toward medicine.
  F: What did chemistry inherit from alchemy?
  B: Distillation, sublimation, crystallization, lab apparatus — and the experimental habit itself.
QUIZ:

     3) Translate languages
  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: The stone is the agent of metallic transmutation and, in parallel, bodily perfection.
  Q: Nigredo, albedo, citrinitas, rubedo are:

     1) Color-stages of the Great Work
     2) Islamic alchemists
     3) Laboratory tools
  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: Blackening, whitening, yellowing, reddening — the work's symbolic sequence.

  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: The Jabirian corpus advanced both laboratory method and sulfur-mercury theory.

MODULE 5: m22-astrology — Astrology — History & Framework
SUMMARY: Four thousand years of celestial interpretation: Babylonian omen-lore, Hellenistic horoscopy, and the symbolic grammar of planets, signs, and houses.
OBJECTIVES:
  - Trace astrology's history from Babylon to the present
  - Name the planets, signs, and houses in the traditional grammar
  - Read a chart's basic structure descriptively
  - Distinguish the tradition's claims from astronomical fact
NOTES:
  [TITLE] The deep history
  **Babylonian** omen literature (2nd millennium BCE, e.g., *Enuma Anu Enlil*) read celestial events as signs for the king and state. **Hellenistic** Egypt (2nd c. BCE–2nd c. CE) invented the **horoscope**: Ptolemy's *Tetrabiblos* systematized planetary meanings still in use. Astrology then traveled through Islamic, Indian (*jyotisha*), Chinese, and medieval European traditions — for most of history practiced alongside astronomy, not against it.
  [TITLE] The symbolic grammar
  Traditional Western astrology combines: **planets** (Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn — plus modern additions Uranus, Neptune, Pluto), each with significations (Mars: drive, conflict); **twelve signs** (Aries–Pisces) as modal qualities; **twelve houses** as life-domains (1st: self, 7th: partnership, 10th: career); and **aspects** (angular relationships, e.g., conjunction, opposition, trine). Meaning emerges from the combination, in the tradition's method.
  [TITLE] How practitioners read a chart
  A **natal chart** maps planetary positions at birth against the houses for a location. Practitioners synthesize: a planet's sign modifies *how* it acts, its house shows *where*, aspects show *relationships* between drives. The tradition presents this as symbolic interpretation of archetypal patterns — practitioners distinguish it from astronomical claims about physical influence.
  [TITLE] The modern status
  Astrology was expelled from the sciences during the 17th–18th centuries; controlled studies have not found effects beyond chance. It persists as a vast interpretive and counseling practice. This course treats it as what it historically is: a symbolic language with four millennia of continuous use — studied for its structure and history, not as verified science.
CARDS:
  F: Astrology's historical stations?
  B: Babylonian omen-lore → Hellenistic horoscopy (Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos) → Islamic/Indian/Chinese/medieval European traditions.
  F: The four components of the traditional grammar?
  B: Planets (what drive), signs (how), houses (where in life), aspects (relationships).
  F: Name the seven classical planets.
  B: Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn.
  F: What is a natal chart?
  B: Planetary positions at birth mapped onto houses for a location — the practitioner's basic document.
  F: The scholarly status of astrological claims?
  B: Expelled from the sciences in the 17th–18th c.; controlled studies find no effects beyond chance.
QUIZ:

     1) The systematization of Hellenistic horoscopic astrology
     2) A modern psychology text
     3) A telescope manual
  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: 2nd-century CE: the classical statement of planetary significations and technique.
  Q: In the traditional grammar, the 10th house signifies:

MODULE 6: m22-tarot — Tarot — History & the Major Arcana
SUMMARY: From 15th-century Italian card game to the 78-card symbolic system: the Major Arcana, the suits, and the Golden Dawn's restructuring.
OBJECTIVES:
  - Trace tarot from game to occult instrument
  - Name and sequence the 22 Major Arcana
  - Describe the four suits and court cards
  - Explain the Golden Dawn's astrological/Qabalistic correspondences
NOTES:
  [TITLE] From game to oracle
  Tarot began as a **15th-century Italian card game** (*tarocchi*) — the 22 trump cards (*trionfi*) plus four suited sequences. Occult reinterpretation started with **Antoine Court de Gébelin** (1781), who claimed Egyptian origins (a claim scholars reject), and was systematized by **Éliphas Lévi** (1850s), who linked the 22 trumps to the Hebrew letters. **Arthur Edward Waite** and **Pamela Colman Smith's** illustrated deck (1909, Rider-Waite-Smith) made the modern symbolic tarot.
  [TITLE] The Major Arcana
  Twenty-two trumps, numbered 0–21: 0 The Fool, I The Magician, II The High Priestess, III The Empress, IV The Emperor, V The Hierophant, VI The Lovers, VII The Chariot, VIII Strength (or Justice), IX The Hermit, X Wheel of Fortune, XI Justice (or Strength), XII The Hanged Man, XIII Death, XIV Temperance, XV The Devil, XVI The Tower, XVII The Star, XVIII The Moon, XIX The Sun, XX Judgement, XXI The World. The sequence is traditionally read as a symbolic journey — the 'Fool's Journey' in modern terms.
  [TITLE] The Minor Arcana
  Fifty-six cards: four suits — **Wands** (fire, will/action), **Cups** (water, emotion/relationship), **Swords** (air, intellect/conflict), **Pentacles** (earth, material/practical) — each with Ace–10 plus four **court cards** (Page, Knight, Queen, King). The Smith illustrations gave every minor card a scene, which is why the Rider-Waite-Smith deck dominates modern practice.
  [TITLE] Golden Dawn correspondences
  The **Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn** mapped the 22 trumps onto the 22 Hebrew letters and the 22 paths of the **Kabbalistic Tree of Life**, and assigned astrological correspondences (e.g., The Emperor–Aries, The Moon–Pisces). This Qabalistic-astrological grid is the engine of most modern tarot interpretation — a 19th-century synthesis, not an ancient inheritance.
CARDS:
  F: Tarot's actual origin?
  B: 15th-century Italian trick-taking game (tarocchi) — occult use begins 1781 with Court de Gébelin.
  F: Name the Major Arcana 0–7.
  B: 0 Fool, I Magician, II High Priestess, III Empress, IV Emperor, V Hierophant, VI Lovers, VII Chariot.
  F: Name the Major Arcana XIII–XXI.
  B: XIII Death, XIV Temperance, XV Devil, XVI Tower, XVII Star, XVIII Moon, XIX Sun, XX Judgement, XXI World.
  F: The four suits and their elements?
  B: Wands–fire, Cups–water, Swords–air, Pentacles–earth.
  F: The Golden Dawn's tarot innovation?
  B: Mapping 22 trumps to Hebrew letters, Tree of Life paths, and astrological correspondences.
  F: The Rider-Waite-Smith deck (1909)?
  B: Waite's system + Pamela Colman Smith's fully illustrated minors — the modern standard.

MODULE 7: m22-enochian — Enochian Magic — Dee & Kelley
SUMMARY: John Dee, Edward Kelley, the angel conversations of the 1580s, and the Enochian system: calls, aethyrs, and the Watchtowers.
OBJECTIVES:
  - Narrate the Dee–Kelley workings and their historical context
  - Describe the Enochian language as the diaries present it
  - Outline the system's structure: Tablets, Calls, Aethyrs
  - Trace Enochian's afterlife in the Golden Dawn and beyond
NOTES:
  [TITLE] Dee and Kelley
  **John Dee** (1527–1609) — mathematician, astronomer, advisor to Elizabeth I — and the scryer **Edward Kelley** conducted 'angelic conversations' (1582–1589), recorded in Dee's detailed diaries (the *Mysteriorum Libri*). Kelley gazed into a shew-stone and dictated; Dee questioned and recorded. The diaries are genuine 16th-century documents — among the most extensive records of Renaissance ritual magic in existence.
  [TITLE] The Enochian language
  The angels dictated a complete language — vocabulary, grammar, and script — which Dee called the language of Adam. Modern linguistic analysis finds it has consistent internal structure (it is a real constructed language, not random syllables), whatever its claimed origin. Practitioners treat it as the system's operative tongue; scholars treat it as Kelley's remarkable linguistic production.
  [TITLE] Structure of the system
  The system as the diaries present it: the **Great Table** (four Watchtowers of elements/directions), the **48 Calls** (or Keys — invocations in the angelic language), the **30 Aethyrs** (expansive spiritual regions), and hierarchies of named angels governing each division. It is the most elaborate angel-magic system in the Western tradition.
  [TITLE] Afterlife
  The Golden Dawn absorbed Enochian wholesale — its Watchtower rituals and grade ceremonies use Dee's Tablets — and through the Golden Dawn it entered Crowley's work (notably the *Vision and the Voice*, his exploration of the Aethyrs) and most subsequent ceremonial magic. Modern Enochian practice descends far more from this 19th-century revival than from Dee's own use.
CARDS:
  F: Who were Dee and Kelley, and when?
  B: John Dee (scholar, Elizabeth I's advisor) and scryer Edward Kelley; angelic workings 1582–1589.
  F: What are the Mysteriorum Libri?
  B: Dee's detailed diaries of the angel conversations — the primary source.
  F: The system's major structures?
  B: The Great Table / four Watchtowers, 48 Calls (Keys), 30 Aethyrs, angel hierarchies.
  F: Scholarly vs. practitioner view of the language?
  B: Scholars: a structured constructed language (Kelley's production). Practitioners: the operative angelic tongue.
  F: How did Enochian reach modern magic?
  B: Via the Golden Dawn's absorption, then Crowley (The Vision and the Voice) and successors.

MODULE 8: m22-rosicrucianism — Rosicrucianism

  The Fama narrates Rosenkreutz's pilgrimage to the East, his founding of the fraternity, and the discovery of his intact tomb 120 years after his death.   The Lutheran pastor **Johann Valentin Andreae** is the leading authorship candidate (he later called the Chymische Hochzeit a youthful 'ludibrium' — a jest). The manifestos belong to the world of early-17th-century reform hopes, Paracelsian medicine, and – soon – the Thirty Years' War that crushed them. Their call for universal reformation resonated with networks that later fed the Royal Society.

Spencer Lewis), still active with its Supreme Grand Lodge, alongside smaller fraternities. Continuity claims between these bodies are traditional assertions, not documented institutional histories.

MODULE 9: m22-theosophy — Theosophy — Blavatsky & the Masters

NOTES:
  [TITLE] Blavatsky and the Society
  **Helena Petrovna Blavatsky** (1831–1891), with Henry Steel Olcott and others, founded the **Theosophical Society** in New York in **1875**. Its three declared objects: universal brotherhood, comparative study of religion and science, and investigation of unexplained laws of nature and human psychic powers. Blavatsky claimed contact with hidden **Masters** (Mahatmas) in Tibet guiding the work.
  [TITLE] The major works
  ***Isis Unveiled*** (1877) attacked materialist science and Christian orthodoxy while defending occult phenomena. ***The Secret Doctrine*** (1888) presented a vast cosmology: seven **root races** cycling through planetary rounds, Fohat and cosmic evolution, all allegedly drawn from a hidden *Book of Dzyan*.   [TITLE] The Masters and the controversies
  The **Mahatmas** (notably Koot Hoomi and Morya) supposedly corresponded with early Theosophists — the famous 'Mahatma Letters.' The honest position: the claims are unproven, the documentary record contested, the movement's influence undeniable.
  [TITLE] Influence
  Theosophy shaped the modern New Age, influenced artists (Kandinsky, Mondrian, Yeats), fed Indian nationalism (Annie Besant's later leadership), and transmitted Buddhist and Hindu concepts to Western audiences — however garbled. Understanding modern esotericism is impossible without it.

MODULE 10: m22-golden-dawn — The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn

  Founded in London in **1888** by **William Wynn Westcott, Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers, and William Robert Woodman**.   A structured curriculum ascending the Tree of Life: the **Outer Order** (Neophyte 0=0 through Philosophus 4=7, elemental grades) and the **Inner (Second) Order** – the *Rosae Rubeae et Aureae Crucis* (Adeptus Minor 5=6 and above, with the Vault of the Adepti ritual).   The Golden Dawn fused **Qabalah** (the Tree as filing system), **tarot** (mapped to the paths), **astrology**, **Enochian magic** (the Watchtower system), and **Egyptian-flavored ritual** into one curriculum — plus the Middle Pillar, pentagram/hexagram rituals, and tattva vision techniques. Nearly all subsequent Anglophone ceremonial magic descends from this synthesis.

Successor orders (Stella Matutina, Alpha et Omega) carried the system forward; **Israel Regardie's** 1937–1940 publication of the rituals made it public property. Crowley, Dion Fortune, and Wicca all drank from this well.

MODULE 11: m22-grimoires — Ceremonial Magic & the Grimoires
SUMMARY: The Solomonic grimoire tradition: the Key of Solomon, the Goetia, spirit hierarchies, and the logic of ritual evocation.

NOTES:
  [TITLE] What grimoires are
  **Grimoires** are handbooks of ritual magic — the most famous descending from the **Solomonic** tradition attributing wisdom to King Solomon. They promise the operator power over spirits: knowledge, treasure, influence. Manuscripts date mainly from the 14th–17th centuries (printed editions from the 17th), drawing on older Jewish, Christian, and Arabic magical traditions.
  [TITLE] The Key of Solomon
  The ***Key of Solomon* (*Clavicula Salomonis*)**: the classic — preparations (fasting, prayer, vestments), **pentacles**, consecrated tools, and procedures for conjuring spirits to visible appearance. Its elaborate purity requirements frame the magic as religious operation: the magician commands spirits *by divine names*, not by personal power.
  [TITLE] The Goetia
  The ***Ars Goetia*** (first book of the *Lesser Key of Solomon*): **72 demons** in hierarchy — kings, dukes, princes, marquises, counts — each with office, appearance, and seal. The ritual logic: the **circle** protects the operator; the **triangle** constrains the spirit; **divine names** compel obedience. Crowley's 1904 edition (with Mathers) revived it for modern occultism.
  [TITLE] Reading them honestly
  Scholars read grimoires as religious literature reflecting their eras' cosmologies — demonology as systematized anxiety about power. Practitioners variously read the spirits as external entities or as psychological archetypes (the Jungian reading popularized in the 20th century). The course presents both readings as *readings*, not verdicts.

  [TITLE] Nag Hammadi
  In **1945** near Nag Hammadi, Egypt, a sealed jar yielded **thirteen codices** — fifty-two texts including the *Gospel of Thomas*, *Gospel of Philip*, and the *Apocryphon of John*.   [TITLE] The Gnostic myth (reconstructed)
  Scholars reconstruct a common pattern across texts: a transcendent, unknowable God; a fall or error producing the **demiurge** (craftsman-god, often identified with the Old Testament creator); the material world as flawed; **sparks of divine light** trapped in human beings; and salvation through **gnosis** — direct experiential knowledge of one's origin — rather than faith or works. This is the texts' teaching, presented as such.
  [TITLE] Gnosis
  **Gnosis** (Greek: knowledge) means salvific, transformative knowing — acquaintance with the divine, not information about it. The *Gospel of Thomas* (saying 77, in the translators' rendering): the kingdom is 'inside of you, and it is outside of you.' Whether these texts describe historical communities' beliefs or literary theology is debated — the course reports the debate, not a verdict.
  [TITLE] Esoteric Christianity
  Beside Gnosticism runs a Christian esoteric current: **Meister Eckhart** (c. 1260–1328) on the Godhead beyond God; **Jacob Böhme** (1575–1624) on the Ungrund and divine unfolding; the **Philokalia** tradition of Orthodox hesychasm (inner prayer). These remained within (or at the edge of) orthodoxy — contemplative practice rather than alternative cosmology.

  **Vajrayana** ('diamond vehicle') Buddhism — Tibetan Buddhism's esoteric stratum — employs deity yoga, mantra, mandala, and guru transmission (*empowerment/initiation required* in the tradition's own terms). Study the traditions in their contexts first; borrow — if at all — with attribution and understanding.

MODULE 14: m22-divination — Divination Systems
SUMMARY: I Ching, runes, and geomancy: three complete divinatory grammars — how each generates, structures, and interprets its signs.

  - Summarize the runic alphabets and their divinatory use
  - Outline geomancy's figures and generation method

NOTES:
  [TITLE] I Ching
  The ***Yijing* (I Ching, 'Book of Changes')**: 64 **hexagrams**, each of six lines — broken (yin) or solid (yang). Traditionally consulted by **yarrow-stalk** manipulation (later simplified to three coins), producing a hexagram plus changing lines that transform into a second hexagram. Each hexagram carries a judgment, image, and line texts (the Ten Wings commentaries, traditionally attributed to Confucius). Three millennia of continuous use as oracle and wisdom text.
  [TITLE] Runes
  The **Elder Futhark** (24 runes, ~2nd–8th c. CE) was first an alphabet for Germanic languages — memorial stones, not fortune-telling. Divinatory use is largely modern: the **blank rune** (popularized by Ralph Blum's 1982 *Book of Runes*) has no historical attestation. The honest account: historical runology (inscriptions, magic formulas on artifacts) versus modern runic divination — related but distinct.
  [TITLE] Geomancy
  **Geomancy** ('earth divination'): the querent makes 16 rows of random marks; reduction by pairs yields four figures of four lines each (the Mothers), from which Daughters, Nieces, Witnesses, and the Judge are derived by a fixed algorithm. Sixteen possible figures (e.g., *Via*, *Carcer*, *Amissio*), each with planetary and zodiacal attributions. Hugely popular in medieval and Renaissance Europe; the most *algorithmic* of the divinatory arts.
  [TITLE] Comparing the grammars
  I Ching: 64 hexagrams, binary lines, transformation built in. Runes: 24 (+1 modern) discrete signs, cast and read positionally. Geomancy: 16 figures generated by deterministic reduction — the most procedurally rigid. All three share the divinatory logic: **random generation + fixed interpretive grammar = a mirror for reflection**. The course presents them as symbolic instruments, not prediction machines.

MODULE 15: m22-symbolism — Symbolism & Sacred Geometry
SUMMARY: How esoteric traditions use symbols: the cross, serpent, and tree; the golden ratio, the vesica, and the Platonic solids — geometry as contemplative instrument.
OBJECTIVES:
  - Explain how symbols function differently from signs
  - Decode recurring symbols: serpent, tree, cross, circle
  - Describe the golden ratio and where it actually appears
  - Use geometric figures contemplatively, not superstitiously
NOTES:
  [TITLE] Signs vs. symbols
  A **sign** points to one thing (a stop sign). A **symbol** participates in what it represents and carries multiple, inexhaustible meanings — the cross, the serpent, the tree. Esoteric traditions work *with* symbols rather than *through* them: meditation on a symbol is meant to transform the meditator, in the traditions' claims. Reducing a symbol to a single 'meaning' kills it.
  [TITLE] The recurring images
  **The serpent**: wisdom, renewal (shedding skin), danger — from Eden to the caduceus to kundalini. **The tree**: axis mundi joining underworld, earth, heaven — Yggdrasil, the Tree of Life, the Bodhi tree. **The circle**: wholeness, cycles, the self — mandalas, ouroboros. **The cross/quartering**: orientation, the four directions/elements, intersection of vertical and horizontal.
  [TITLE] Sacred geometry
  The **golden ratio** φ = (1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.618: the ratio whose removal of a square leaves a similar rectangle; it appears in the pentagon, phyllotaxis, and – honestly – far fewer artworks than popular books claim. The **vesica piscis** (overlapping circles) generates the equilateral triangle; the five **Platonic solids** (known to the Greeks, proved complete by Euclid) anchor cosmological speculation from Plato's *Timaeus* to Kepler's *Mysterium Cosmographicum* (1596).
  [TITLE] Honest geometry
  The contemplative use of geometry — drawing, meditating on proportion — is a genuine traditional practice. The *claims* about φ encoding the pyramids or governing all beauty are mostly modern myth; debunking them doesn't touch the practice. Keep the two separate: the discipline is real, the hype is not.
CARDS:
  F: Sign vs. symbol?
  B: Sign: points to one thing. Symbol: participates in its referent, inexhaustibly multi-meaning.
  F: Serpent, tree, circle, cross: core resonances?
  B: Serpent: wisdom/renewal/danger. Tree: axis mundi. Circle: wholeness/cycles. Cross: orientation, fourfold order.
  F: φ: definition and value?
  B: (1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.618 — self-similar rectangle ratio; in the pentagon and phyllotaxis.
  F: The five Platonic solids; who proved completeness?
  B: Tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron — Euclid proved these are all.
  F: The honest stance on φ hype?
  B: Most art/architecture claims are modern myth; the contemplative geometric practice stands apart from the hype.
QUIZ:
  Q: The vesica piscis is formed by:
     0) Three tangent circles
     1) Two overlapping circles of equal radius
     2) A square in a circle
     3) A pentagram
  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: The almond-shaped intersection — the generator of equilateral-triangle geometry.
  Q: Kepler's Mysterium Cosmographicum (1596) used:
     0) The Platonic solids nested to model planetary orbits
     1) The golden ratio to date fossils
     2) Tarot for astronomy
     3) Nothing geometric
  ANSWER: 0 | EXPLAIN: Kepler's early model nested the five solids between planetary spheres — wrong, but gloriously geometric.
  Q: A symbol differs from a sign in that it:
     0) Is simpler
     1) Carries inexhaustible, participatory meaning
     2) Is always religious
     3) Cannot be drawn
  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: The inexhaustibility is the point — meditation on symbols vs. reading of signs.
  Q: The ouroboros (serpent eating its tail) symbolizes, in the traditions' reading:
     0) Danger only
     1) Cyclical renewal and wholeness
     2) A specific deity
     3) Alchemical failure
  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: The self-consuming circle: eternity, return, the unity of beginning and end.

MODULE 16: m22-modern-revival — The Modern Occult Revival & Critical Reading
SUMMARY: Crowley, Fortune, Gardner, and the New Age: the 20th century's occult explosion — and how to read esoteric claims critically without contempt.
OBJECTIVES:
  - Narrate the 20th-century occult revival's key figures
  - Describe Wicca's founding and Thelema's core tenet
  - Apply source criticism to esoteric texts
  - Hold the emic/etic balance in practice
NOTES:
  [TITLE] Crowley and Thelema
  **Aleister Crowley** (1875–1947) — Golden Dawn initiate, mountaineer, prolific author — received (he claimed, via his wife Rose) ***The Book of the Law*** in Cairo in 1904, founding **Thelema**: 'Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law' — where 'will' means the True Will, one's authentic nature/purpose, not whim. The **A∴A∴** and **Ordo Templi Orientis** (O.T.O.) carried the system. Crowley's influence on 20th-century occultism is unmatched; his biography remains the field's most contested.
  [TITLE] Fortune, Gardner, and Wicca
  **Dion Fortune** (1890–1946): *The Mystical Qabalah* (1935) — the clearest English Qabalah textbook – and the Society of the Inner Light. **Gerald Gardner** (1884–1964) founded **Wicca** in the 1940s–50s, claiming initiation into a surviving witch-cult; scholars (notably Ronald Hutton) find Wicca to be a mid-20th-century creation drawing on Crowley, Freemasonry, and folklore — now a genuine lived religion regardless of origins.
  [TITLE] The New Age and the library
  From the 1970s, the **New Age** movement popularized — and diluted — every tradition in this course: channeling, crystal healing, 'quantum' mysticism. The scholarly study of esotericism simultaneously professionalized (the Amsterdam chair in Hermetic philosophy, 1999; the ESSWE society). Both are facts about the same decades: popularization and academization together.
  [TITLE] Critical reading without contempt
  The course's closing discipline: **source criticism** (who wrote this, when, from what sources, for whom?), **claim sorting** (historical claim vs. symbolic claim vs. experiential claim — each judged by its own standards), and **charity** (the strongest version first). Neither credulity nor debunking-as-sport: the traditions deserve the same rigor you'd give any human phenomenon — and no more credulity than you'd give any extraordinary claim.
CARDS:
  F: Thelema's central tenet and its source?
  B: 'Do what thou wilt' — True Will, not whim; from The Book of the Law (Cairo, 1904).
  F: Crowley's orders?
  B: A∴A∴ and the O.T.O. (Ordo Templi Orientis).
  F: Dion Fortune's key work?
  B: The Mystical Qabalah (1935) — the classic English Qabalah textbook; Society of the Inner Light.
  F: Scholarly consensus on Wicca's origins?
  B: Mid-20th-century creation (Gardner, 1940s–50s) from Crowley, Freemasonry, folklore — per Hutton; a genuine lived religion regardless.
  F: The three critical-reading moves?
  B: Source criticism; claim sorting (historical/symbolic/experiential); charity — strongest version first.

     0) A new occult order
     1) The academization of esotericism studies
     2) A publishing house

  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: The professionalization of the field alongside its popularization.
  Q: Claim sorting means:
     0) Believing all claims
     1) Judging historical, symbolic, and experiential claims each by appropriate standards
     2) Rejecting all claims
     3) Sorting books by size
  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: A historical claim faces archives; a symbolic claim faces coherence; an experiential claim faces phenomenology.
  Q: Hutton's finding on Gardnerian Wicca:
     0) Stone-age survival proven
     1) A mid-20th-century creation, now a genuine lived religion

  ANSWER: 1 | EXPLAIN: Origins debunked as ancient survival; the religion's reality as lived practice affirmed.
