# Batch B — write to batch-B.json — 8 modules (IL29-M09 through IL29-M16)
Theme: ThreatLocker operations + MIT 6.858 part 1.

M09 | ThreatLocker Portal Administration | Organizations, computers, agents, policies, reports — the admin's daily loop; agent health monitoring; deployment states; reporting for compliance conversations. Lab: design a portal organization structure for a 3-site MSP client base (fictional).
M10 | Cyber Hero Support Operations | The support/triage role: ticket intake, severity classification, escalation paths, communication under pressure; what "hero" support means operationally (fast, accurate, documented). Ties to the Oct 13 interview — this is the team he'd join.
M11 | Policy Troubleshooting & Break-Fix | When allowlisting breaks a legitimate app: the diagnosis workflow (identify the blocked action, check the policy, test the exception, document); common breakage patterns (updaters, installers, macros); the change-control discipline for exceptions. Lab: break-fix runbook with 5 realistic scenarios.
M12 | ThreatLocker Deployment Projects | Rollout planning: pilot rings, Learning Mode baselining windows, stakeholder communication, rollback plans, success metrics; a 90-day deployment plan for a 200-endpoint fictional company.
M13 | MIT 6.858: Threat Models & Security Architecture | What a threat model is (assets, adversaries, attack surface, trust boundaries); the 6.858 approach of reasoning from first principles; applying threat modeling to an endpoint fleet. Worked example: threat-model a small business file server.
M14 | MIT 6.858: Control Hijacking & Buffer Overflows | Stack layout, return addresses, how overflows hijack control flow — taught conceptually with worked examples; defenses: canaries, DEP/NX, ASLR; why allowlisting makes exploitation harder even when a bug exists.
M15 | MIT 6.858: Privilege Separation & Capabilities | Least privilege in software design; splitting programs into privileged/unprivileged parts; capabilities as unforgeable tokens; how these ideas show up in Elevation Control and Ringfencing.
M16 | MIT 6.858: Sandboxing & Isolation | Sandboxing native code; seccomp, containers, VMs as isolation layers; the performance/security tradeoff; mapping sandboxing concepts onto Ringfencing policies.
