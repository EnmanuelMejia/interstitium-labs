## Learning Outcomes (objectives)
1. Explain the transformer architecture and why attention scaled.
2. Describe what quantum computers can and cannot do, and the post-quantum migration logic.
3. Summarize CRISPR's mechanism and the state of gene-editing applications.
4. Evaluate fusion energy claims against demonstrated physical results.
5. Compare BCI approaches and their current capabilities.
6. Reason about AI agents, inference-time compute, and the task-time horizon.
7. Explain semiconductor scaling challenges beyond Moore's law.
8. Survey synthetic biology and space infrastructure developments.
9. Read capability-forecasting work (Epoch AI corpus) with appropriate skepticism.
10. Apply the Frontier Method: cruxes, demonstrated results, and updating from evidence.

## Noah AI Integration (required)

> Noah AI is the core learning-experience driver for this course — not a bolt-on. Every module is taught with Noah as the Socratic tutor: learners work through the material in conversation with Noah, which is course-aware (grounded in this course's modules, notes, labs, quizzes, and drills), adapts with SM-2 spaced repetition, and speaks with 5 natural voices for a voice-first experience. Learners can talk with Noah via voice chat while working through modules — asking questions, working problems aloud, and getting challenged back before answers are given.

**Driver:** Noah drives the learning experience: guided Socratic walkthroughs of each module, Socratic questioning before answers, drill generation from the learner's weak spots, and lab debriefs that connect hands-on work back to the module's objectives.

**Personas → modules:**

- **Visionary** — Lives here: extrapolates the curves — what transformers, fusion, and BCIs imply for the next decade. _(modules: m23-transformers, m23-fusion, m23-bci, m23-epoch-ai)_
- **Engineer** — Grounds the hype in mechanisms: how attention actually works, what a qubit actually does. _(modules: m23-transformers, m23-quantum, m23-semiconductors)_
- **Philosopher** — Asks the hard questions the papers skip: what would change your mind, what is the crux? _(modules: m23-frontier-method, m23-metr-evals)_
- **Mentor** — Teaches the frontier method itself: how to read a fast-moving field without drowning. _(modules: m23-frontier-method, m23-epoch-ai)_
- **Trickster** — Plays the hype merchant: pitches the breathless press release, then makes you find the buried caveat. _(modules: m23-fusion, m23-crispr, m23-synbio)_

**Voices:** 5 natural voices. Five natural voices. Voice-first: learners talk with Noah via voice chat while working through modules — hands free for labs, terminals, and notebooks.

**Adaptive:** SM-2 spaced repetition. Noah schedules flashcard and drill reviews with the SM-2 algorithm, prioritizing cards and modules the learner gets wrong. Forgetting curves are fought per-learner, not per-cohort.

**Course-aware:** Noah tutors from the course itself — every explanation, hint, and drill references the module content the learner is on. No generic answers; everything is grounded in this course.

**Frontier note:** The frontier moves fast — papers, benchmarks, and capability curves update monthly. Noah's course-aware tutoring keeps learners current: it flags where a module's snapshot may have moved on, teaches the frontier method for evaluating new claims, and drills the skill of updating beliefs from new evidence rather than memorizing a frozen snapshot.
