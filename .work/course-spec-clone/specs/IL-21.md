## Learning Outcomes (objectives)
1. Distinguish the branches of philosophy and state what philosophy is for.
2. Analyze and construct arguments: premises, inferences, objections, fallacies.
3. Apply the three ethical traditions (utilitarian, deontological, virtue) to technology decisions.
4. Reason about responsibility for autonomous and AI-mediated systems.
5. Explain how scientific knowledge is made and revised (epistemology, philosophy of science).
6. Narrate the history of science and technology from antiquity to the present.
7. Write clear technical prose: structure, precision, revision.
8. Argue persuasively and ethically: rhetoric for real rooms.
9. Apply economic reasoning (costs, incentives, game theory) to engineering decisions.
10. Read law and policy as a technologist: literacy, not legal advice.

## Noah AI Integration (required)

> Noah AI is the core learning-experience driver for this course — not a bolt-on. Every module is taught with Noah as the Socratic tutor: learners work through the material in conversation with Noah, which is course-aware (grounded in this course's modules, notes, labs, quizzes, and drills), adapts with SM-2 spaced repetition, and speaks with 5 natural voices for a voice-first experience. Learners can talk with Noah via voice chat while working through modules — asking questions, working problems aloud, and getting challenged back before answers are given.

**Driver:** Noah drives the learning experience: guided Socratic walkthroughs of each module, Socratic questioning before answers, drill generation from the learner's weak spots, and lab debriefs that connect hands-on work back to the module's objectives.

**Personas → modules:**

- **Visionary** — Connects the humanities to the technologist's future — why philosophy and history compound into judgment. _(modules: m21-what-is-philosophy, m21-synthesis)_
- **Engineer** — Treats arguments as systems: premises in, conclusions out — debug the inference like code. _(modules: m21-logic-argument, m21-technical-writing)_
- **Philosopher** — Home turf: the three ethical traditions, epistemology, philosophy of science — Socratic dialogue at full depth. _(modules: m21-ethics-1, m21-ethics-2, m21-epistemology)_
- **Mentor** — Coaches writing and rhetoric: drafts, feedback, revision — the craft of being understood. _(modules: m21-technical-writing, m21-rhetoric)_
- **Trickster** — Steelmans the other side: argues the position you despise better than you can, then asks you to beat it. _(modules: m21-rhetoric, m21-ethics-2, m21-economics-2)_

**Voices:** 5 natural voices. Five natural voices. Voice-first: learners talk with Noah via voice chat while working through modules — hands free for labs, terminals, and notebooks.

**Adaptive:** SM-2 spaced repetition. Noah schedules flashcard and drill reviews with the SM-2 algorithm, prioritizing cards and modules the learner gets wrong. Forgetting curves are fought per-learner, not per-cohort.

**Course-aware:** Noah tutors from the course itself — every explanation, hint, and drill references the module content the learner is on. No generic answers; everything is grounded in this course.
