## Learning Outcomes (objectives)
1. Prove statements with direct, contrapositive, contradiction, and induction methods.
2. Count with permutations, combinations, and recurrence relations.
3. Operate fluently with vectors, matrices, eigenvalues, and linear transformations.
4. Differentiate and integrate single-variable and multivariable functions; apply the Fundamental Theorem.
5. Test series for convergence and work with power series representations.
6. Model uncertainty with probability: Bayes, distributions, expectation.
7. Apply statistical inference: estimation, hypothesis testing, regression.
8. Solve linear systems and formulate optimization problems (Lagrange, gradient reasoning).
9. Verify mathematical claims computationally with Python (SymPy, numeric methods).
10. Read and write mathematics at the level the technical courses (IL-03–IL-08, IL-12, IL-16, IL-17) assume.

## Noah AI Integration (required)

> Noah AI is the core learning-experience driver for this course — not a bolt-on. Every module is taught with Noah as the Socratic tutor: learners work through the material in conversation with Noah, which is course-aware (grounded in this course's modules, notes, labs, quizzes, and drills), adapts with SM-2 spaced repetition, and speaks with 5 natural voices for a voice-first experience. Learners can talk with Noah via voice chat while working through modules — asking questions, working problems aloud, and getting challenged back before answers are given.

**Driver:** Noah drives the learning experience: guided Socratic walkthroughs of each module, Socratic questioning before answers, drill generation from the learner's weak spots, and lab debriefs that connect hands-on work back to the module's objectives.

**Personas → modules:**

- **Visionary** — Shows where the mathematics leads — how linear algebra becomes ML, how calculus becomes optimization. _(modules: m20-linear-algebra-1, m20-calculus-1, m20-linear-systems-optimization)_
- **Engineer** — Makes you compute: works problems step by step, checks your algebra, never lets a hand-wavy step pass. _(modules: m20-linear-algebra-2, m20-calculus-2, m20-numerical-methods)_
- **Philosopher** — Asks what a proof really is — why rigor matters, what 'obvious' is hiding. _(modules: m20-proof-methods, m20-sets-logic)_
- **Mentor** — Paces the long road: study habits, readiness checks, when to push and when to review. _(modules: IL-20-M00, m20-series)_
- **Trickster** — Plants the classic traps: the broken induction, the divergent series that looks convergent, the 'obvious' false step. _(modules: m20-proof-methods, m20-series, m20-probability)_

**Voices:** 5 natural voices. Five natural voices. Voice-first: learners talk with Noah via voice chat while working through modules — hands free for labs, terminals, and notebooks.

**Adaptive:** SM-2 spaced repetition. Noah schedules flashcard and drill reviews with the SM-2 algorithm, prioritizing cards and modules the learner gets wrong. Forgetting curves are fought per-learner, not per-cohort.

**Course-aware:** Noah tutors from the course itself — every explanation, hint, and drill references the module content the learner is on. No generic answers; everything is grounded in this course.
